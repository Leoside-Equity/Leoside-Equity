4. What a valuation stance means

Reports on a single American or British company carry a valuation stance (Undervalued, Fairly valued or Overvalued), a fair value band and a horizon. These describe how the market price on the date of writing compared with the author's own estimate of value. They are observations about price, not forecasts and not recommendations.

  • Undervalued means the price was below the author's estimate of value. It does not mean buy.
  • Overvalued means the price was above it. It does not mean sell, and it does not mean the company is a poor business.
  • Fairly valued means the two were close. It does not mean hold.
  • A fair value band is a range produced by a model from assumptions that may prove wrong. It is not a price target.
  • A horizon is the period the assumptions were framed around. It says nothing about when, or whether, any price will move.

A stance reflects a view held on the date of publication, may change without notice, and will not necessarily be updated or withdrawn. Always check the date on a report before treating it as current.

5. Indian coverage

Reports on India cover the market as a whole or a sector. They carry no valuation stance, fair value band or horizon, and they do not give a view, recommendation or price target on any individual security listed in India. Views on a market or a sector are opinions about many businesses at once, which makes them broader, not safer. They are not predictions or signals to act.
